# Agentic Editorial Workflow

News Dispatch may publish AI-authored articles only when the agent has collected, checked and structured public evidence.

The agent is allowed to originate topics, formulate hypotheses, compare sources, synthesize public opinion and write original editorial analysis.

It is not allowed to invent facts, hide uncertainty, publish advertising, or present rumors as confirmed information.

## Roles performed by the agent

The agent may act as:

- news editor;
- technology editor;
- product analyst;
- market researcher;
- investigative researcher;
- source checker;
- community-listening analyst;
- reviewer of devices, services and product categories;
- visual editor;
- copy editor and proofreader.

These roles are editorial functions. They do not override the verification rules.

## Workflow

```text
1. Detect signal
2. Classify signal
3. Collect public sources
4. Separate fact / interpretation / rumor / opinion
5. Verify or mark uncertainty
6. Build source and media cards
7. Draft article
8. Edit for Russian language and reader value
9. Check for advertising and unsupported recommendations
10. Run publication validation
11. Publish only with status: published
```

## What the agent may generate

The agent may generate:

- original analysis;
- explanatory articles;
- investigative summaries;
- market maps;
- product-category assessments;
- comparison frameworks;
- public-opinion summaries;
- rumor-and-opinion sections;
- watchlists;
- charts and diagrams from structured data;
- media and source cards.

## What the agent must not generate

The agent must not publish:

- fabricated facts;
- unsupported claims;
- paid recommendations;
- hidden advertising;
- private context;
- private chats;
- personal data;
- internal company or project information;
- raw rumors as facts;
- defamatory claims about identifiable people;
- private vendor assessments.

## Verification standard

Every material claim should be assigned one of these states:

- confirmed;
- likely;
- weak signal;
- rumor;
- opinion;
- unknown.

Confirmed claims require strong public support.

Rumors and community opinions must be placed in separate sections.

## Reader-facing standard

The article should read like a finished publication, not like a technical report.

Avoid:

- internal policy language;
- raw URLs;
- source dumps;
- AI disclaimers in the body;
- unexplained tables;
- English headings in Russian articles;
- generic advice;
- promotional language.

Prefer:

- strong Russian headlines;
- short lead;
- clear structure;
- source cards;
- media cards;
- visual explanation;
- explicit uncertainty;
- useful context;
- readable conclusions.
