# Publication Boundary

Everything committed to this repository must be safe for public disclosure.

This rule applies even when the repository is private.

Git history, branches, issues, pull requests, comments, deleted files, file names, commit messages, and metadata can become visible later. Therefore, the repository must never be used for raw private notes, internal analysis, sensitive drafts, personal inventories, company-sensitive work, or unredacted context.

## Public-by-default rule

If a detail cannot appear on a public website, it must not enter this repository.

Do not commit first and redact later.

## What belongs here

- Anonymized editorial standards.
- Public-safe templates.
- Public-source-based dispatches.
- Source policies.
- Taxonomies.
- Static-site files.
- Privacy and validation tooling.
- Publishable visual assets without sensitive metadata.

## What stays outside

- Personal calibration notes.
- Private prompts.
- Raw research notes containing sensitive context.
- Internal product, company, client, partner, contractor, roadmap, metric, or strategy details.
- Private financial information.
- Home or work infrastructure specifics.
- Private documents, screenshots, emails, chats, calendars, or account data.
- Secrets, tokens, credentials, private URLs, IP addresses, hostnames, and operational topology.

## Editorial anonymization rule

The dispatch may be calibrated for a specific reader or group, but the published text must be readable as independent public analysis.

Do not name the intended beneficiary.

Do not write:

- for our product;
- for our company;
- for our team;
- for teams building X;
- for operators of Y;
- for product teams in category Z.

Prefer neutral analytical phrasing:

- the signal may indicate;
- the likely implication is;
- the observable mechanism is;
- the pressure may increase around;
- the position changes if;
- the hypothesis should be tested against.

## Commit messages

Commit messages must also be public-safe.

Do not mention private projects, private plans, private counterparties, or sensitive work context in commit messages.
