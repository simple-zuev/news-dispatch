# Style Guide

News Dispatch should read like a mature analytical publication, not a chatbot answer.

## Voice

- Analytical.
- Adult.
- Clear.
- Dense but readable.
- Source-aware.
- Non-promotional.
- Non-directive unless a risk is confirmed and critical.

## Avoid

Avoid generic phrases without mechanism or criteria:

- important to watch;
- do not buy on hype;
- check reviews;
- the future is here;
- this changes everything;
- good things reduce friction;
- this may be useful for our team;
- for product teams building X.

## Prefer

- the signal may indicate;
- the mechanism is;
- the second-order effect may be;
- the position changes if;
- the hypothesis should be tested against;
- a useful observation criterion is;
- the main uncertainty is;
- this remains a weak signal until.

## Structure

Write in layers:

1. What changed.
2. Why it is not just a news item.
3. What mechanism matters.
4. What follows if the signal is real.
5. What would falsify the view.
6. What decision criteria follow.

## Recommendations

Recommendations should not expose an intended beneficiary.

Bad:

> For teams building regulated digital asset infrastructure...

Better:

> The signal may indicate a broader shift toward provable operating controls, stronger audit trails, and clearer user-facing disclosure.

Bad:

> Our product should consider stronger onboarding.

Better:

> A relevant evaluation criterion is whether onboarding patterns increasingly combine user suitability, disclosure clarity, monitoring evidence, and documented exception handling.

## Formatting

Use:

- short sections;
- tables for comparisons;
- bullet points only when they add scanability;
- clear source classes;
- explicit confidence markers;
- separate facts from interpretation;
- visual cards and diagrams only when they add analytical value.

## Headline style

Headlines should state a thesis, not only a topic.

Weak:

> AI PC news

Stronger:

> Local AI is a margin shift, not a laptop feature

Weak:

> Regulatory update

Stronger:

> Compliance pressure is moving from declarations to evidence trails

## Public-safe language

The text must remain useful without revealing private calibration.

Use neutral categories:

- the category;
- the market;
- the infrastructure layer;
- the operating model;
- the control surface;
- the distribution channel;
- the user trust layer;
- the vendor landscape.
