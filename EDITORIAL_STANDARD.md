# Editorial Standard

News Dispatch is an analytical publication system, not a news clipping service.

## Required level

Every dispatch should read like a senior editorial intelligence memo.

Avoid shallow advice such as:

- `watch this trend`;
- `do not buy on hype`;
- `check reviews`;
- `this is the future`;
- `good products reduce friction`.

These statements are only acceptable when followed by concrete conditions, mechanisms, thresholds, evidence, trade-offs, and falsifiable criteria.

## Editorial formula

Each significant item should follow this analytical chain:

```text
Signal
-> Verification
-> Context
-> Mechanism of influence
-> Second-order effects
-> Decision criteria
-> What would change the view
-> New knowledge
```

## Signal classification

Every important claim should be classified as one of:

- fact;
- release;
- research;
- review;
- benchmark;
- user review;
- community signal;
- rumor;
- forecast;
- marketing claim;
- editorial inference.

## Confidence levels

Use confidence labels:

- `high` — directly supported by reliable primary or multiple independent sources;
- `medium` — plausible and supported, but incomplete or early;
- `low` — weak, early, community-only, or rumor-level;
- `unknown` — insufficient evidence.

## Non-obvious analysis requirements

For each key signal, try to answer:

1. Why is this not merely a news item?
2. What mechanism changes behavior, markets, products, costs, or decisions?
3. Who benefits?
4. Who loses or absorbs new risk?
5. What is the leading indicator?
6. What is the lagging indicator?
7. What should be checked in 7/30/90 days?
8. What evidence would change the current position?
9. What does this teach beyond the immediate topic?

## Dispatch structure

Recommended issue structure:

1. Issue panel
2. Executive brief
3. Main editorial essay
4. Key signals
5. Community radar
6. Buying and material-culture radar
7. Finance and consumer environment
8. Horizon notes
9. Signal vs noise
10. Risks and limits
11. Decisions with criteria
12. Change in worldview

## Writing style

The style should be:

- adult;
- precise;
- dense but readable;
- editorial, not assistant-like;
- analytical, not promotional;
- concrete, not generic;
- privacy-safe;
- source-aware.

## Good vs bad examples

Bad:

> Do not buy AI PCs yet; wait for reviews.

Better:

> The relevant decision is not whether AI PCs are a real category, but whether local workloads create enough value to offset higher device cost, heat, memory pressure, and OS-level privacy complexity. The position changes if independent tests show stable local execution of useful models under realistic battery, noise, and workflow constraints.

Bad:

> EDC gear should reduce friction.

Better:

> A carry item is justified only if it reduces the cost of a recurring action more often than it increases weight, cognitive overhead, and retrieval complexity. The evaluation unit is not feature count; it is repeated scenario value per gram and per cubic centimeter.

Bad:

> Check community reviews.

Better:

> Community evidence should be classified by pattern type: isolated complaint, repeated defect, firmware regression, bad batch, compatibility issue, long-term durability signal, or subjective preference. Only repeated and mechanism-consistent patterns should affect a buying position.
