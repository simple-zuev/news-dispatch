## 3. Regulatory and restriction layer

A publishable dispatch should separate confirmed regulatory events from draft texts, media interpretations, and weak signals. It should avoid legal conclusions unless they are directly supported by public sources.

## 4. Technology and infrastructure layer

Relevant observations may include public evidence of changes in custody disclosure, monitoring logic, audit trails, role separation, identity checks, data residency claims, and operational resilience patterns.

## 5. Market and competitor layer

Public competitor signals should be treated as category-level observations, not as internal strategic instructions.

## 6. Vendor and integration landscape

Only public vendor documentation, public announcements, public tenders, public hiring signals, or public technical artifacts may be used.

## 8. Misread risk

| Wrong reading | Better reading |
|---|---|
| A public vendor claim proves market maturity | Vendor claims should be tested against adoption evidence and implementation detail |
| A draft regulatory signal is already an obligation | Drafts should be tracked separately from enacted rules |
| A competitor feature implies a required response | Public features should be interpreted through mechanism, adoption, and regulatory relevance |

## 9. Data gaps and limits

This sample has no current sources and must not be used as analysis. A real dispatch should identify source gaps, weak confidence areas, and what would change the view.
